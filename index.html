An Extraordinary Story

A Journey from the Brink of Existence

Written with unexpected humour, mysticism, and fearless honesty, Philip Watling chronicles his recovery from catastrophic trauma to lifelong purpose.

"I was hit by a car and killed. Thankfully, death wasn't terminal."

Crossing the road on his way to work with horses, Philip was struck by a car and thrown head-first against a bus. Suffering severe traumatic brain injury and massive physical trauma, his heart stopped beating twice.

Through the extraordinary skill of the Royal London HEMS air ambulance team, months across three intensive care hospitals, and years of arduous rehabilitation in Milton Keynes, Philip didn’t just survive—he rediscovered the power of kindness, friendship, and peace.

Meet the Author

A Life Dedicated to Caring and Community

Philip Watling graduated with a Bachelor of Science (Honours) in Zoology & Ecology from Queen Mary and Westfield College, University of London. After university, he pursued his passion for working with horses before his life changed forever in 1994.

Today, Philip lives in Milton Keynes where he devotes his time to supporting an autistic adult, volunteering at the local community fridge, and speaking with individuals facing recovery and trauma.

Book Excerpt

Chapter 1: The Crossing

From "Flight of a Lifetime: Miracles Do Happen" by Philip Watling

In December 1994, I had what most would consider the dream setup. Having completed my degree at Queen Mary and Westfield College in London, I was working with horses—a life full of vitality, physical energy, and direction.

Then came the morning of December 9. Crossing the road on my routine walk to work, the world shifted in an instant. A car collided with my legs, launching me onto the bonnet and dislocating my shoulder. As the brakes locked, the momentum hurled me head-first into the side of a passing bus.

My Glasgow Coma Score fell to 5. Clinically, I died. My heart stopped. But in what can only be called a miraculous intersection of rapid response from the Royal London HEMS air ambulance crew and pure tenacity, death wasn’t terminal...

This book is not a tale of sorrow; it is the story of how surviving that day allowed me to rebuild my personality, discover the goodness in human community, and laugh even when circumstances seemed impossible.
